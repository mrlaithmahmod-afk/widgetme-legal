# UK Market Size and Buyer Segments for an AI Property Visualisation SaaS (as of October 2026)

Scope: floor plans to 3D floor plans, CGI renders, virtual tours, videos and buyer configurators. The UK is the primary market; the UAE, Saudi Arabia, the EU and the US are covered briefly. Figures are in GBP unless stated. **[EST]** marks the researcher's own estimates or assumptions. **[HYP]** marks hypotheses, each with a proposed test. Everything else has a cited source.

---

## Q1. How many organisations are there in each segment?

### Takeaway
Estate agency is by far the largest segment by count: about 25,700 ONS-registered businesses and 16,385 Rightmove agency branches. The developer segments are small and concentrated: roughly 2,000 to 2,500 SME builders, a few hundred medium builders, about 25 nationals and 2,887 developments advertised on Rightmove. There are 3,769 RIBA Chartered Practices, about 6,400 interior design businesses and a BTR sector of 310k homes. I found no official counts for property marketing agencies or BTR operators.

### Cited Findings
**Estate agents (A, B, C)**
- There were **25,665 active UK estate agency businesses in 2025** (ONS data, SIC 68.31), up 2% from 25,155 in 2024. A forecast puts 2026 at 26,705 — [Estate Agent Today / PropertyDriveBuy analysis of ONS](https://www.estateagenttoday.co.uk/breaking-news/2025/12/agent-numbers-set-to-grow-by-4-in-2026-forecast/). IBISWorld gives 26,374 businesses in 2026 — [IBISWorld](https://www.ibisworld.com/united-kingdom/number-of-businesses/estate-agents/3845/). The ONS also publishes counts by size band — [ONS ad hoc 2447](https://www.ons.gov.uk/businessindustryandtrade/business/activitysizeandlocation/adhocs/2447estateagentsbysizeandregion). Caveat: SIC 68.31 includes lettings-only and one-person businesses.
- About 29% of UK agencies are in London, 15% in the South East and 10% in the East of England — [Estate Agent Today / GetAgent (2024)](https://www.estateagenttoday.co.uk/breaking-news/2024/01/estate-agency-businesses-on-the-rise-despite-branch-closures-research/).
- Rightmove had **16,385 agency branches** at 31 Dec 2025 (16,124 in 2024), with 90% retention and "agent formation … the highest on record". It had **2,887 New Homes developments** (2,923 in 2024) and 19,272 advertisers in total — [Rightmove FY2025 results (LSE RNS)](https://www.londonstockexchange.com/news-article/RMV/annual-financial-report/17479517).
- Online and hybrid agents had a 5.2% share of exchanges in 2025, and self-employed agents 2.2% — [TwentyCi via The Intermediary](https://theintermediary.co.uk/2026/01/property-sales-and-transactions-reach-decade-high-in-2025-twentyci/).
- About **126,000 UK properties are valued at £2m or more**. This is a proxy for the prime/luxury stock (C) — [TwentyCi End of Year 2025](https://www.twentyci.co.uk/phmr/twentyci-property-homemover-report-end-of-year-2025/).

**Housebuilders (D, E, F)**
- NHBC registrations by size band, 1988 → 2014: builders of **1–10 units/yr fell from 10,112 to 2,000**, 11–30 units from 1,500 to 244, and 31–100 units from 603 to 159. Firms building fewer than 500 units/yr registered just over a quarter of homes in 2015, down from two-thirds in 1988 — [FMB written evidence to Parliament citing NHBC](https://committees.parliament.uk/writtenevidence/71466/html/).
- Recent SME counts conflict: United Trust Bank says "around 2,500" SMEs today — [UTB](https://www.utbank.co.uk/wise-owls/reversing-the-decline-of-sme-home-builders/). WPI Strategy (2025) says "fewer than 1,200" active SME housebuilders in England — [WPI Strategy SME report 2025](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/6363e5f35ce88928f09e6a5e/t/6842f5d5835d8f45022447c4/1749218876850/WPI+Strategy+SME+Builders+Report+2025.pdf). The two use different definitions and geographies (UK vs England, active vs registered).
- NHBC covers "70%+ of all new homes built in the UK" — [NHBC new home statistics review 2025](https://www.nhbc.co.uk/kontentdocuments/584b0061-1ef7-49a8-b820-894270c63fa8/NHBC%20new%20home%20statistics%20review%202025.pdf). Counts based only on NHBC therefore undercount builders who use other warranty providers.
- Among small builders surveyed by NHBC, almost 60% develop one site at a time. 64% expected to build 1–10 homes and 15% none (2016) — [NHBC Foundation, Small house builders and developers](https://www.nhbc.co.uk/kontentdocuments/6d97b78c-0f03-40b7-b8ff-6acec08927de/small-house-builders-and-developers.pdf).
- Builders of more than 2,000 homes/yr built 63% of the homes built by housebuilders in 2017 (NHBC) — [Savills](https://www.savills.co.uk/research_articles/229130/267515-0). This is the latest concentration figure I found.
- HBF SME State of Play 2025/26 (Nov 2025): more than half of SMEs feel less optimistic than a year ago, and planning delays remain the top barrier — [HBF](https://www.hbf.co.uk/research-insight/state-of-play-2025/). The SMEs surveyed say they could build an extra 100,000 homes (+56%) — [Close Brothers summary](https://www.closepropertyfinance.com/news-and-insights/state-play-2025-unlocking-sme-potential-deliver-100000-more-homes).

**Architects (I)**
- **3,769 RIBA Chartered Practices** (sum of the 2025 size bands). By FTE: 1 person 971; 2 people 544; 3–<5 619; 5–<10 819; 10–<20 410; 20–<50 268; 50–<100 85; 100+ 53. Staff total 42,000 and revenue is about £5bn. Private housing is 81% of revenue for one-person practices, 58% for 5–<10 and 22% for 100+. The average number of projects per year runs from 28 (1 person) to 343 (100+) — [RIBA Benchmarking 2025 Executive Summary](https://www.riba.org/media/br1k2otq/riba-benchmarking-2025-executive-summary.pdf).
- Small practices (1–9 staff) are 78% of practices but only 13% of revenue. Large practices (50+) are 4% of practices and 61% of revenue — [RIBA Journal](https://www.ribaj.com/intelligence/riba-business-benchmarking-survey-2025/).

**Interior designers (J)**
- **6,415 interior design businesses in the UK in 2026**, with a market size of £1.8bn — [IBISWorld](https://www.ibisworld.com/united-kingdom/industry/interior-design-activities/14549/). The BIID estimates about 20,000 practising interior designers (May 2021). The Design Council counted about 39,000 architecture, built-environment and multidisciplinary design firms in 2017 — [BIID](https://biid.org.uk/news/how-many-interior-designers-are-there-uk).

**BTR (K)**
- The UK BTR sector is **310,310 homes, of which 156,688 are completed** (Q2 2026). Annual starts **fell 79% to 3,455**, and homes under construction fell 21% year on year — [RE:UK / Savills BTR Q2 2026](https://realestateuk.org/our-work/reuk-build-to-rent-report-q2-2026/). The 12 Core Cities pipeline was about 108,000 units at Q1 2026 — [Savills Q1 2026](https://www.savills.com/research_articles/255800/389260-0).

### Inferences
- **[EST]** Split of agency branches: about 65% independent (~10,650 branches, perhaps 8,000 firms at about 1.3 branches each) and about 35% in multi-branch or corporate groups (~5,735 branches, perhaps 200–300 groups). The ONS business count (25.7k) is well above the branch count (16.4k) because it includes letting-only, sole-trader and non-portal firms.
- **[EST]** C (luxury/prime): about 300 firms or prime-focused offices, covering national prime brands, London boutiques and country-house agents.
- **[EST]** D (small developers, 1–10 units/yr): about 2,000 firms across all warranty providers. This sits between the WPI and UTB figures and the NHBC 2014 base of 2,000.
- **[EST]** E (medium, ~11–2,000 units/yr): about 500 firms (NHBC 2014: 244 + 159 in the 11–100 bands, plus regional builders of 100–2,000 units).
- **[EST]** F (large nationals, 2,000+ units/yr): about 25 groups.
- **[EST]** G (off-plan sellers): about 200 organisations — London and regional city-centre developers plus international off-plan selling agents. Molior says 56 halted sites are "one in five" of London developments, which implies about 280 London private-for-sale developments of 20+ homes under construction — [Progressive Research summarising Molior](https://progressive-research.com/insights/alu-economic-london-residential-development-woes-news-26bn-naval-rebuild-programme-fortnight-ahead/).
- **[EST]** H (property marketing / CGI agencies): about 200. **[EST]** K (BTR operators and investor-developers): about 120.

### Gaps
- I found no authoritative count of independent vs corporate branches. Test: scrape Rightmove's agent directory or buy TwentyCi branch data.
- No current (2024–26) NHBC builder counts by size band were public. The NHBC Pro data service has them — [NHBC FAQ](https://www.nhbc.co.uk/frequently-asked-questions/details?category=data_services&subcategory=residential_construction_statistics).
- No count of UK property marketing agencies or BTR operators. Test: Companies House SIC 73110/74100 with keyword filters; UKAA/BPF membership lists.
- Propertymark membership figures were not retrieved.

## Q2. What volumes do these segments handle (completions, developments, off-plan share, listings)?

### Takeaway
Each year there are about 1.7m sales listings and about 1.2m transactions. England completes about 190k new-build homes, and 2,887 new-homes developments advertise on Rightmove. London off-plan is in a historic slump: Q3 2026 private sales were the lowest since Q1 2009, with record unsold stock.

### Cited Findings
- **Over 1.7m properties were listed for sale in 2025**, the highest in 10 years and up 2.1%. Sales agreed exceeded 1.25m, and time to sale agreed averaged 77 days, the longest in nine years — [TwentyCi End of Year 2025](https://www.twentyci.co.uk/phmr/twentyci-property-homemover-report-end-of-year-2025/); [The Intermediary](https://theintermediary.co.uk/2026/01/property-sales-and-transactions-reach-decade-high-in-2025-twentyci/).
- There were 1.21m UK transactions in 2025, against an average of 1.20m since 2015 — [Rightmove FY2025](https://www.londonstockexchange.com/news-article/RMV/annual-financial-report/17479517). July 2026 HMRC residential transactions were 96,710 (seasonally adjusted), 1% below July 2025 — [Mortgage Introducer](https://www.mpamag.com/uk/mortgage-types/residential/uk-home-sales-slip-in-july-but-market-direction-remains-positive/587835).
- **England: 190,600 new-build completions in 2024-25** (198,680 in 2023-24) and 208,600 net additions, down 6% — [MHCLG net additional dwellings 2024-25](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/housing-supply-net-additional-dwellings-england-2024-to-2025/housing-supply-net-additional-dwellings-england-2024-to-2025).
- In the year to Dec 2025, starts were 124,860 (+15%). Q4 2025 completions were 36,720 (seasonally adjusted). An estimated 191,300 net additions were delivered from 1 Apr 2025 to 15 Mar 2026 — [MHCLG indicators of new supply Oct–Dec 2025](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/housing-supply-indicators-of-new-supply-england-october-to-december-2025/housing-supply-indicators-of-new-supply-england-october-to-december-2025).
- NHBC registered 26,959 new homes in Q1 2026, down 6% on Q1 2025 (28,715) — [NHBC](https://www.nhbc.co.uk/insights-and-media/news/new-home-registrations-down-in-q1).
- 2,887 New Homes developments advertised on Rightmove at end-2025 — [Rightmove FY2025](https://www.londonstockexchange.com/news-article/RMV/annual-financial-report/17479517).
- **London (Molior, schemes with 20+ private homes):**
  - 8,436 new homes sold in 2025.
  - Q4 2025: 2,463 sales, of which 1,014 to individuals and 1,449 to companies.
  - 37,388 homes under construction; work stopped on 5,009 homes at 51 sites.
  - Source: [Molior Jan 2026](https://www.moliorlondon.com/media/molior-report-media-2026/01/molior-residential-development-in-london-january-2026.pdf).
  - Q1 2026: 2,850 sales, of which only 907 to individuals. About 50% of the 36,750 homes under construction are already sold, and 15,100 under construction still need buyers — [Molior Apr 2026](https://www.moliorlondon.com/media/molior-report-media-2026/04/molior-residential-development-in-london-april-2026-final.pdf).
  - H1 2026: 5,606 sales (-37% year on year) and a record 4,629 complete but unsold — [Evening Standard](https://www.standard.co.uk/news/london/london-new-build-home-sales-houses-property-b1292587.html).
  - **Q3 2026: 1,440 private sales, below Q1 2009.** About 7,160 starts and about 7,070 sales in the first three quarters. Delivery is forecast at about 10,000 private homes a year — [Knight Frank, 14 Oct 2026](https://www.knightfrank.co.uk/research/article/2026/10/the-gulf-between-ambition-and-delivery-in-the-london-new-homes-market).
  - Berkeley builds about 16% of London's private homes, Vistry and London Square about 5% each — [Progressive Research](https://progressive-research.com/insights/alu-economic-london-residential-development-woes-news-26bn-naval-rebuild-programme-fortnight-ahead/).

### Inferences
- **[EST]** About 104 sales listings per Rightmove branch per year (1.7m ÷ 16,385), excluding lettings.
- **[EST]** About 190k completions across 2,887 marketed developments implies roughly 65 homes sold per development per year.
- About half of London's under-construction private stock is pre-sold, so off-plan selling remains structural in London. Its volume, however, has collapsed.

### Gaps
- I found no national (non-London) figure for the share of new builds sold off-plan.
- Lettings listing volumes are not quantified; TwentyCi reports only +9.3% rental supply.
- I found no figure for the total number of active NHBC sites.

## Q3. What do these segments currently spend on visualisation?

### Takeaway
Spend splits into three tiers:
- **Estate agent per-listing items:** £5–£119 per floor plan and £100–£350 per tour.
- **Developer CGI packages:** £1k–£8k per small or medium scheme, £3.5k–£12k per fly-through, and £1.8k–£4.5k per verified view.
- **Show homes:** £50k–£150k+ each.

Developers' sales and marketing budgets are benchmarked at 1.5–4% of GDV.

### Cited Findings
**Estate agent floor plans and tours**
- Metropix: £44.90/month + VAT for 20 2D plans; £59.75/month with 20 3D upgrades included; AutoDraw from £5.95 per plan — [Landmark Metropix pricing](https://www.landmark.co.uk/products/metropix/pricing/); [Metropix 2D](https://www.landmark.co.uk/products/metropix/2d-floorplans/).
- Metropix costs about £5–£15 per plan, and professional services £15–£40 per plan at 1–5 plans a month — [Photoplan 2026](https://www.photoplan.co.uk/blog/best-floor-plan-software-for-estate-agents).
- Outsourced plans: 2D £30, colour 2D £60, 3D floor plan £90 per property — [Fenridge](https://fenridge.co.uk/estate-agents/).
- CubiCasa adds large-property surcharges of £20–£135 above 465 m² — [CubiCasa](https://help.cubi.casa/en/articles/15557089-large-property-fees).
- Online agents charge vendors £20–£119 for floor plans as an add-on — [OnlineAgentPicker](https://onlineagentpicker.com/).
- 360 tours cost £100–£200 and Matterport scans £150–£350, plus hosting — [Photoplan virtual tour guide 2026](https://www.photoplan.co.uk/guides/how-much-do-virtual-tours-cost).
- Matterport UK plans: Starter £10/month, Professional £48/month, Business £243/month (annual billing, June 2026) — [See3D](https://www.see3d.ai/blog/posts/matterport-pricing-uk-2026).
- Agency tour retainers run £70–£110 per property — [The Virtual Tour Company](https://thevirtualtour.company/residential-estate-agent-pricing). Matterport tours from £129 up to 1,000 sq ft — [Steele London](https://www.steele.london/virtual-tours).

**Developer CGI and animation**
- Residential exterior still £450–£900; pair of interiors £600–£1,200; commercial package £2,500–£6,000; verified view £1,800–£4,500; 30–60s fly-through £3,500–£12,000 — [VisuArchitect](https://www.visuarchitect.com/questions/3d-architectural-visualization/how-much-does-3d-visualisation-cost/).
- Planning render £350–£600; planning package £1,500–£4,000; **marketing launch package £3,000–£8,000**; interactive walkthrough from £3,000. "Repeated identical units are nearly free" — [Blockout Digital](https://www.blockoutdigital.com/guides/how-much-does-architectural-cgi-cost).
- CGIs for a typical small development £1,000–£5,000; day rates £250–£500; fly-throughs take 4–6 weeks — [iCreate](https://icreate.co.uk/cgi-price-list/).
- Exterior from £350, interior from £450, animation from £3,500, aerial/masterplan from £1,500 — [Shakworks](https://www.shakworks.com/architectural-visualisation-services).
- Low end: exterior £345, interior £245, 60s video £1,650–£2,295 — [Real Render 3D](https://www.realrender3d.co.uk/). Stills £550–£800 — [Resolution Studios](https://www.resolution-studios.co.uk/post/how-much-will-a-3d-visualisation-cost).

**Developer marketing budget as % of GDV**
- Council viability benchmarks put marketing at **3% of GDV (of which 1% is agent fees)**. Another borough quotes 1.5–3%, with the higher end for larger schemes with show flats — [GLA EIR disclosure](https://www.london.gov.uk/who-we-are/governance-and-spending/sharing-our-information/freedom-information/foi-disclosure-log/eir-gla-benchmarks-development-viability-reviews).
- Avison Young's Argus appraisals use **2.5% of GDV** for sales and marketing — [Braintree Local Plan evidence](https://www.braintree.gov.uk/downloads/file/724/exd062-5of5-appraisal-summaries-and-worksheets-oct-19).
- An SME developer guide uses 3–5% (4% in its worked example) — [Bellco](https://bellcoconstruction.co.uk/residual-land-valuation-sussex-kent/). This is an industry blog, so lower weight.

**Show homes and sales suites**
- A show home costs about £50k to build, fit out, power and staff, versus a virtual show home "as little as £2.5k". This is vendor marketing — [Pixel](https://pixel-future.com/2022/05/27/reduce-showhome-build-and-fit-out-costs-by-95-by-going-virtual/).
- Show homes cost £50k to £150k+ per unit — [Impact Aerial 2026](https://www.impactaerial.co.uk/2026/03/10/virtual-tour-for-new-build-homes-the-developers-guide-to-immersive-marketing-in-2026/). Older London range: £20k to £1m — [The Times 2014](https://www.thetimes.com/life-style/property-home/article/show-homes-keep-new-build-sales-rising-096brdwsd69?region=global).
- A bespoke portable marketing suite is listed at £99,500 — [KC Modular](https://kcmodularbuildings.co.uk/product/portable-marketing-suite/).
- Staging costs £5k–£12k per unit physical vs £150–£300 virtual — [Featherington Interiors](https://www.featheringtoninteriors.com/post/multi-unit-property-staging-the-developer-s-guide-to-uk-projects). This is a stager's blog; its "HBF says" quotes could not be verified, so lower weight.

**Interior designers**
- A freelance render costs $200–$1,500, typically $350–$800 per furnished room — [Monaverse 2026](https://monaverse.com/blog/3d-rendering-cost-interior-design). This is US-oriented.

### Inferences
- **[EST]** Typical development: 50 homes × £350k = £17.5m GDV. At 2.5–3% that is £440k–£525k of sales and marketing, of which about 1–1.5% is agent fees. That leaves about £175k–£350k for non-agent marketing, while visualisation (a £3k–£8k CGI package plus a £3.5k–£12k fly-through) is about £7k–£20k. Visualisation is therefore a small share of the marketing budget, and the show home is the large line item an interactive configurator could partly substitute.
- **[EST]** Independent agent visualisation spend per branch: about £1,000/yr (floor plans only, about £10 × 104) to about £6,000/yr (outsourced plans plus tours on a quarter of listings). I use a midpoint of £3,000/yr.

### Gaps
- No UK survey of agent annual spend on floor plans or tours was found. No public Rightmove rule requiring floor plans was found.
- No breakdown of large housebuilder CGI budgets was found. National builders reuse house-type CGIs, so their spend per site is likely lower.

## Q4. What do these buyers already pay for software and services (willingness-to-pay benchmarks)?

### Takeaway
Agents already pay Rightmove £1,530 per branch per month and new-homes advertisers £2,135 per development per month. They pay only about £45–£60 per office per month for floor-plan software. A visualisation tool's price therefore anchors at low hundreds of pounds per branch per year for agents and low tens of thousands per scheme for developers. Prices for the new product are hypotheses until tested.

### Cited Findings
- Rightmove FY2025 ARPA: **Agency £1,530/month** (+6%), **New Homes £2,135/month** (+7%), total £1,621. Revenue £425.1m — [Rightmove FY2025](https://www.londonstockexchange.com/news-article/RMV/annual-financial-report/17479517). H1 2025 Agency ARPA was £1,520 — [Rightmove H1 2025](https://plc.rightmove.co.uk/content/uploads/2025/07/H1-2025-RNS.pdf).
- Metropix £44.90–£59.75 per office per month — [Landmark](https://www.landmark.co.uk/products/metropix/pricing/). Made Snappy in-house 360 tours £49 per branch per month, versus outsourced tours of at least £100 each; one lettings agent cut tour spend from about £600/month to under £50 — [Property4Media](https://property4media.com/showPRPreview/100082251).
- Photoplan's illustration: an instruction is worth about £4,200 in fees, and a £150–£180 tour is about 4% of that — [Photoplan](https://www.photoplan.co.uk/guides/how-much-do-virtual-tours-cost).
- RIBA micro-practice economics: average revenue of £55.9k for a 1-person practice and £109.7k for a 2-person practice, "before costs such as … software" — [RIBA Journal](https://www.ribaj.com/intelligence/riba-business-benchmarking-survey-2025/). Benchmarks from Photoplan's comparison: SketchUp from £100/yr, AutoCAD from £200/month, RoomSketcher from £30/month — [Photoplan](https://www.photoplan.co.uk/blog/best-floor-plan-software-for-estate-agents).

### Inferences — WTP hypotheses (no price below is observed for this product)
- **[HYP] A/B agents: £50–£150 per branch per month.** Anchors: Metropix (£45–£60) and Made Snappy (£49). That is about 3–10% of Rightmove ARPA. *Test:* Van Westendorp survey plus a price-tier landing page with 30–50 independents; measure paid conversion at £59, £99 and £149.
- **[HYP] C prime agents: £150–£500 per listing**, or £400–£1,000 per office per month. Anchored on CGI virtual staging at £245–£450 per interior image and Matterport at £300–£500. *Test:* paid pilot with 5 prime offices, comparing per-listing and seat pricing.
- **[HYP] D small developers: £500–£2,500 per project.** This is 30–60% of the £1k–£5k studio price. *Test:* offer AI CGI packs at £750 and £1,500 alongside studio quotes and track win rate.
- **[HYP] E/G developers and off-plan sellers: £5k–£25k per scheme per year** for CGI, tour, video and configurator. Anchors: a £3k–£8k launch package plus a £3.5k–£12k fly-through, and Rightmove New Homes at about £25.6k per development per year. *Test:* 3 paid pilots measuring cost per reservation versus a studio package.
- **[HYP] F nationals: £50k–£250k per group per year** as an enterprise licence. *Test:* discovery with 3 group sales and marketing directors; ask for current house-type CGI and options budgets.
- **[HYP] I/J architects and interior designers: £30–£100 per user per month.** *Test:* freemium funnel conversion.

### Gaps
- Reapit, Alto and Street CRM prices per branch per month were not found in public sources.
- No observed prices for AI-native competitors were gathered.

## Q5. How do 2025–2026 UK market conditions affect urgency and budgets?

### Takeaway
2025 was a decent resale year (1.2m transactions and record agent formation). In 2026 the Middle East (Iran) conflict pushed inflation to 3.1% and fixed mortgage rates to about 5.4–5.9% with base rate held at 3.75%, and markets price in hikes. The London new-build market is at post-2009 lows. Pressure to sell is high for developers and off-plan sellers, but their budgets are tight. Agents face rising competition, which favours cheap differentiation tools.

### Cited Findings
- Bank Rate was held at 3.75% on 17 Sep 2026; CPI was 3.1% and expected to rise because of energy prices from the Middle East conflict. The next decision is due 5 Nov 2026 — [Bank of England](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/monetary-policy/the-interest-rate-bank-rate). The vote was 6–3, with three members voting to raise — [BCIS](https://www.bcis.co.uk/news/bank-of-englands-latest-decision-on-base-rate/).
- The average 2-year fix was 5.83% and the 5-year 5.87% on 17 Sep 2026 (Moneyfacts) — [BBC](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/c3dky111m40o). Rightmove's figure is about 5.39%, up from 4.25% before the Iran war — [Rightmove](https://www.rightmove.co.uk/news/articles/property-news/base-rate-held-sept26/).
- Mortgage lending fell in August 2026, and markets expect two rate rises within six months — [BSA Oct 2026](https://www.bsa.org.uk/media-centre/bsa-blog/october-2026/market-update-october-2026).
- Building Safety Regulator: after reforms in June 2025, the BSR reported 7,089 starts in Q4 2025, against a previous quarterly maximum of 1,206 — [MHCLG](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/housing-supply-indicators-of-new-supply-england-october-to-december-2025/housing-supply-indicators-of-new-supply-england-october-to-december-2025). London starts were up 82% year on year in Q2 2026 "as backlogs at the Building Safety Regulator eased" — [Progressive Research](https://progressive-research.com/insights/alu-economic-london-residential-development-woes-news-26bn-naval-rebuild-programme-fortnight-ahead/).

### Inferences

## Q8. Which three segments are most attractive?

### Takeaway
1. **E Medium residential developers.** High spend per organisation (£7k–£20k per scheme plus show-home substitution), owner or director decision-makers, 1–3 month cycles, and high urgency from slow absorption and cost pressure. This is the largest SAM per customer outside enterprise.
2. **G Off-plan sellers.** The most urgent segment: London sales are at a post-2009 low, a record 4,629 homes are complete but unsold, and 15,100 homes under construction still need buyers. CGI, fly-through and configurator are central to selling unbuilt homes, and per-scheme budgets are the highest. The risk is a small, concentrated universe with tight budgets.
3. **C Luxury and prime agents.** High value per listing, a brand-driven need to stand out, short cycles and low price sensitivity. They also provide credible reference logos that cascade down to A and B.

A (independent agents) is the largest pool by count. It is best treated as a later self-serve volume play because of low ACV and high churn. H (marketing agencies) should be explored as a white-label channel into E, F and G.

### Cited Findings
- Off-plan urgency: [Knight Frank Oct 2026](https://www.knightfrank.co.uk/research/article/2026/10/the-gulf-between-ambition-and-delivery-in-the-london-new-homes-market); [Evening Standard / Molior](https://www.standard.co.uk/news/london/london-new-build-home-sales-houses-property-b1292587.html); [Molior Apr 2026](https://www.moliorlondon.com/media/molior-report-media-2026/04/molior-residential-development-in-london-april-2026-final.pdf).
- Developer budgets: [GLA EIR](https://www.london.gov.uk/who-we-are/governance-and-spending/sharing-our-information/freedom-information/foi-disclosure-log/eir-gla-benchmarks-development-viability-reviews); [Blockout](https://www.blockoutdigital.com/guides/how-much-does-architectural-cgi-cost); [Impact Aerial](https://www.impactaerial.co.uk/2026/03/10/virtual-tour-for-new-build-homes-the-developers-guide-to-immersive-marketing-in-2026/).
- Prime stock: [TwentyCi](https://www.twentyci.co.uk/phmr/twentyci-property-homemover-report-end-of-year-2025/).

### Inferences
- Run E and G together as a "developer and off-plan" go-to-market: same buyer persona (Sales & Marketing Director) and same product bundle (CGI, tour, video, configurator).
- G's budgets are contracting. Price per scheme against avoided studio and show-home cost rather than as additive spend.

### Gaps
- Ranking weights (spend, urgency, cycle, churn) are the researcher's judgement. They should be validated with pilot conversion data.

## Q9. What do the expansion markets (UAE, Saudi Arabia, EU, US) signal?

### Takeaway
Dubai is the strongest expansion signal: an off-plan-dominated, record market with hundreds of project launches a year. Saudi ROSHN is a large, digitally led pipeline. I did not research the EU and US in depth.

### Cited Findings
- **Dubai 2025** (DLD): 214,912 sales transactions worth AED 682.5bn (+18.9% volume). About 203,000 residential sales, with **off-plan 62.6% of transactions (134,623 sales, ~AED 293bn)** — [Zawya / DLD](https://www.zawya.com/en/press-release/research-studies/dubai-real-estate-market-hits-aed-682.5bln-with-214912-transactions-in-2025-371494). There were **446 off-plan project launches** in 2025 — [Christie's Dubai](https://www.christiesrealestatedubai.com/the-journal/dubai-breaks-real-estate-records-in-2025/). Cavendish Maxwell puts the off-plan share at 73% (up from 69%) — [Developers News](https://developersnewsmagazine.com/dubais-residential-property-marketsecures-record-breaking-200000sales-worth-aed541-5-billion-in2025-says-cavendish-maxwell/). Property Monitor's adjusted off-plan share was 72.1% (July 2025) — [Property Monitor](https://propertymonitor.com/insights/monthly-market-report/monthly-market-report-july-2025). The share differs by methodology.
- **Saudi ROSHN:** mandate of 400,000 homes by 2030 and about 85,000 in the pipeline — [AGBI tracker](https://www.agbi.com/saudi-giga-projects/roshn/). About 155,000 planned across its communities (Marafy 52,000; Almanar 33,000; Sedra 30,000) — [Knight Frank Saudi Giga Projects](https://www.knightfrank.com.sa/site-assets/research/report-pdfs/saudi-arabia-giga-projects-002025.pdf). **78% of ROSHN's 2025 deals were made via its app** — [AGBI Jan 2026](https://www.agbi.com/analysis/construction/2026/01/giga-project-roshn-says-most-sales-made-via-app/).

### Inferences
- **[EST]** Dubai's 446 annual off-plan launches are comparable to the whole UK E+G universe (about 700 organisations), with a far higher off-plan share. That makes it the natural second market for the developer and off-plan product.
- Regional conflict risk in 2026 (Iran) may affect Gulf demand; I did not quantify this.

### Gaps
- EU and US market sizing was not researched. Suggested sources: NAR membership (~1.5m US agents), US new-home sales from the Census Bureau, and EU new-build completions from Eurostat.
- I found no 2026 Dubai off-plan data.
